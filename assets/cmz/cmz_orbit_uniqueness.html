Why orbits uniquely map to location in the Galactic Center

Stars in the Milky Way's nuclear stellar disc mostly move on x2 orbits: closed, nested ellipses elongated perpendicular to the Galactic bar (Nieuwmunster et al. 2024). Because these orbits are non-crossing and their speed varies with position, the line-of-sight velocity you'd measure along a fixed sightline changes as a distinctive function of distance — unlike for simple circular orbits, where near and far points along the same sightline look kinematically identical. That asymmetry is what lets proper motions + orbit models break the classic near/far distance ambiguity (the method behind arXiv:2609.10782's 3D dust map).
