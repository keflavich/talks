The MUBLO: 160 km s−1 lines and no known counterpart

Ginsburg+ 2024 (discovery) · ALMA images: Ginsburg+ in prep · bottom row: JWST NIRCam 2.12 / 4.8 μm and ALMA CS 2–1
Top row: new ALMA images at 0.07-0.09" (11-14x finer than the discovery data): 3 mm, 850 um and 450 um continuum show a compact core plus an arc offset by 0.33" (~2700 au), seen in all three bands; SO 3(2)-2(1) is the line emission (bigger beam). Bottom row: JWST NIRCam 2.12 um and 4.8 um, a 4.8/2.12 composite, and CS 2-1. At 4.8 um the MUBLO is a dark patch against the diffuse background -- it is in front of the Galactic-center starlight, so it absorbs. There is a 4.8 um point source near the crosshair; whether it has anything to do with the MUBLO is NOT established -- the line is "no counterpart identified so far".

The magenta crosshair in each panel marks the MUBLO's catalog position (RA 266.4905821, Dec -28.9529931, ICRS; the discovery position from Ginsburg+ 2024), which is also the center of every cutout -- not a fitted continuum peak, and not any near-IR candidate. The core + arc structure means the brightest pixel need not sit on it.

The Millimeter Ultra-Broad-Line Object, G0.02467−0.0727, found in ACES 3 mm data (Ginsburg+ 2024, ApJL 968, L11). Published: continuum spectral index ~3.3 (dust); CS, SO and SO2 lines with FWHM ~160 km/s and a Gaussian-looking profile; emitting area <~1" (<~10^4 au); v_LSR ~40-50 km/s, so it is in the Galactic center; LTE temperature from several SO lines 13 K (low-density, subthermally excited warmer gas not excluded); no SiO, so no strong (>~10 km/s) shocks; no detection at X-ray, infrared or radio wavelengths. The paper weighed eight kinds of explanation; most are inconsistent with the data or do not fully explain them -- it called the MUBLO "observationally unique".

Top: new ALMA images at 0.07-0.09" (11-14x finer than the discovery data): 3 mm, 850 um and 450 um continuum show a compact core plus an arc offset by 0.33" (~2700 au), seen in all three bands; SO is the line emission (bigger beam). Lower left: the SED -- a dust-like millimeter rise (ALMA points, with modified blackbodies at 10-25 K for scale) and only upper limits (Spitzer, ISO backgrounds) everywhere else. The mm spectral index of the core alone is ~2.4 and the continuum fluxes are PRELIMINARY -- I have not put them on the slide. Excitation temperature ~13-14 K.

Chemistry: seven transitions of four hydrogen-free sulfur species are detected (E_u = 7-88 K), while eleven hydrogen-bearing species have only upper limits; N(HCN)/N(CS) is ~22-28x below the surrounding cloud. It looks hydrogen-poor, which no standard picture (protostellar outflow, evolved star, ISM feature) accommodates comfortably.

No counterpart has been identified at any wavelength shorter than ~1 mm, so we do not know what powers it. The JWST Cycle 6 proposal (submitted, not awarded) asks for spectroscopy: hydrogen recombination lines (Br-alpha) and a near-IR counterpart would separate a stellar/accretion origin from a hydrogen-depleted ejection. 1" = 8200 au at 8.2 kpc. Do not quote a mass.

Sgr B2 Deep South: “fluffy” 25 μm emission, source unknown

left: MIRI F2550W / F1280W / F770W (R/G/B)  ·  right: F2550W alone, the boxed region
  • Patchy, flocculent emission ~30″ (~1.2 pc) across, roughly circular, starting at the edge of Sgr B2 DS
  • Only apparent at 25 μm; not detected by Spitzer or SOFIA
  • The rough symmetry hints at central feedback — but nothing obvious would make this morphology
Budaiev+ 2026, AJ 172, 51, Fig. 9
From Budaiev+ 2026, "Extended flocculence in Sgr B2 DS": extended 25 um emission in part of Sgr B2 DS, flocculent, with identifiable local peaks and missing patches. Parts of it are visible in F1280W, F770W and Br-alpha, but extremely faint and indistinguishable from other large-scale features; the structure is only apparent at 25 um (their Fig. 9 caption). Not detected by SOFIA at 25 and 37 um or Spitzer at 24 um, likely because of sensitivity and resolution. It begins near the edge of Sgr B2 DS and extends for 30" in a circular shape (30" = 1.2 pc at their 8.277 kpc; the panel's scale bar is 5" = 0.2 pc). The top side is dimmer -- either no emission there, or foreground material extincting it. In their words: "We do not know what drives this unique morphology"; the rough symmetry hints at a central feedback-driven mechanism, "there is no obvious configuration that would produce such a flocculent morphology", and the source of the emission is unclear.

The dashed gray circles in the right panel are sources affected by F2550W persistence (their appendix), not features. The third panel of the paper figure (not shown here) is the same field in Br-alpha: filaments and stars, not the same patches.

Not to be confused with the other 25 um result in the same paper: around Sgr B2 N, 25 um emission follows the large-scale SiO outflow, which they read as infrared radiation escaping the protocluster along the outflow cavities. That one has an explanation; this one does not. The same figure's left panel also shows the sharp eastern edge of Sgr B2 DS.

Small dark blobs in a MIRI “control” field (Budaiev+)

MIRI F770W
  • Compact, opaque spots against faint diffuse 7.7 μm emission
  • In a field picked as a control: ~0.6° above the midplane, away from the CMZ clouds
  • What makes clumps this small opaque at 7.7 μm — and how far away are they?
JWST GC Treasury (GO 10678), MIRI F770W parallels · Budaiev+ in prep
The JWST GC Treasury has two NIRCam pointings in an inner-Bar control field about half a degree north of the plane; their MIRI F770W parallels land ~6' east, at l ~ 0.00-0.10, b ~ +0.59-0.64 (~85 pc above the midplane in projection at 8.2 kpc). In the left one (zoom, right panel) there is a scatter of small, dark, opaque-looking spots against the faint diffuse emission (Budaiev+ in prep).

What I measured myself in the 2026-09-13 F770W mosaic (not from Nazar -- check before quoting): seven compact deficits in this 48" zoom at >4 sigma below a 61-pixel median background, ~0.2-1.2 arcsec^2 each (a few pixels to ~1" across), 7-11 MJy/sr deep against a ~40-50 MJy/sr surface; centers at (l, b) = (0.0756, 0.6121), (0.0685, 0.6125), (0.0731, 0.6126), (0.0659, 0.6129), (0.0688, 0.6146), (0.0692, 0.6147), (0.0701, 0.6151). Several look elongated or tailed.

Why it is odd: dust that is dark at 7.7 um has to be optically thick-ish in the mid-infrared, i.e. a lot of column in an arcsecond-sized spot, and this field was chosen precisely because it is off the CMZ's molecular clouds. We do not know their distance (foreground disk, Bar, or Galactic center) or their nature.

Caveats: I have not checked individual exposures, so persistence or bad-pixel clusters are not formally excluded; the dark notch at the field edge just outside the zoom is an edge artifact, and the dark-centered bright stars are saturated cores.

State of the art, 2024-2026 (all checked in the arXiv sources):
- H.E.S.S. 2026 (Acharyya+ 2026, A&A accepted, arXiv:2608.25946 -- the refereed version of Devin+ 2025; ~365 h, 2004-2020): a 3D likelihood fit (l, b, E). The gas is the Tsuboi+ 1999 CS(1-0) cube, each velocity band assigned the line-of-sight distance from Sawada+ 2004's face-on map -- assuming CS and CO are distributed alike, with "the limited line-of-sight (z axis) resolution of 30 pc" of Sawada's map. A 1/r^alpha cosmic-ray profile times that gas gives alpha = 1.10 +- 0.05 stat +- 0.10 syst (MAGIC 2020: 1.2 +- 0.3; H.E.S.S. 2016: 1.10 +- 0.12): the ridge "is well reproduced with the assumed gas distribution model".
- New in the refereed paper: (a) the ridge spectrum curves (>3 sigma) -- parent protons break at ~15-50 TeV (or cut off at 60-160 TeV), so "PeVatron" needs care; HAWC's emission to 100 TeV favors curvature over a cutoff. (b) They concede that for Sgr B2 and the 20/50 km/s clouds "the 30 pc resolution of Sawada's model appears limiting and leads to unrealistic dispersion along the line of sight". (c) Run in reverse, assuming stationary injection at the GC: Sgr C closer than ~90 pc along the line of sight is disfavored, "thus putting the Twisted Ellipse and Open Streams models in tension"; the 20/50 km/s clouds act like compact clouds 20-35 and 40-55 pc from the GC (not >~100 pc, as in Sofue's two arms); the Dust Ridge within +-60 pc; Sgr B2 unconstrained (only a 2% flux change). They call these "qualitative" and close: CTAO "might be able to ... place stronger constraints on 3D CMZ models". Note the circularity: the distances come from the same 1/r fit the gas model was used to derive.
- HAWC 2024 (ApJL 973, L34): the PeVatron confirmed beyond 100 TeV; gas from the same CS map (5e6 Msun in the H.E.S.S. regions).
- Ren+ 2025 (A&A 697, A42; MPIK): a new CO + dust (Herschel, Planck) column and X_CO analysis for the CMZ -- and the quote: the lack of a reliable total gas column density model is still the limiting factor.
- Models: Dorner+ 2024 (ApJ 965, 180; 3D anisotropic transport) use Ferriere+ 2007 -- with its scale height raised from 18 to 30 pc to match the gamma-ray thickness -- and note the "lack of substructures within the gas distribution"; Becker Tjus+ 2022 (CRPropa) add discrete clouds (Sgr B2, the dust ridge) and a 3D field to Ferriere; Scherer+ 2022-2024 use disk or ring CMZ toys traced by CO; Lai+ 2026 (PRD) propose neutrinos to test the gas tracers, noting CS and dust disagree significantly. CTAO's LST-1 has begun GC observations (Abe+ 2025).
- The 2018 H.E.S.S. line is still worth quoting: "Since there is no reliable 3D model of the gas distribution in the CMZ, we do not model the ridge emission with a physically motivated CR density."
So the gas side is a 1999 CS cube plus a 2004 face-on map -- before the orbit models, ACES, or JWST.

Projected distance ≠ distance from Sgr A*

γ-ray flux ∝ ∫ nCR ngas dℓ: each H.E.S.S. region is a strip through the CMZ, and the gas in it can sit 60–150 pc from the accelerator even when it is 15 pc away on the sky.

The point for the next two slides: w_CR is gamma-ray luminosity over gas mass. Nothing in it depends on where a cloud sits along the line of sight (beyond the ~8 kpc distance in the luminosity). Only the x-axis assumes a geometry, and the paper says so: comparing the points with the profile "requires assuming that the average distance of the molecular cloud complexes to the GC is comparable to their projected distance on the plane of the sky", adding that their gas model "suggests this approximation is acceptable for most of the clouds considered".

A power law fit at projected distance, to the five clouds that every geometry places (MC50, MC20, the Bridge, Sgr C, Sgr B2; all three tracers, with the tracer spread folded into the errors), gives alpha = 0.84 +- 0.08, chi2 3.1/3. That is shallower than their 1.10 from the full 3D fit, as it should be if the inner clouds are farther from Sgr A* than they look.

One thing to flag, not claim: for MC20 the gamma rays prefer 20-35 pc, while Nogueras-Lara+ 2026 put it at least 56 +- 11 pc in front (a lower limit). If both hold, steady injection (or the 30% tolerance) is under strain.

- Ferriere+ 2007, the community's own 3D model: alpha = 1.10 (0.99-1.19), chi2 10.6/8 -- the same.
- the Kruijssen, Dale & Longmore 2015 stream (uniform mass per unit length; it reaches 8 of the 10 regions, not the two outermost at l = 0.94 and 1.14): the gas-weighted distances are 63-69 pc for the inner three annuli and 77-117 pc for the circles, so the steep drop in w across the annuli needs alpha = 1.60 (1.30-1.92), and the fit is poor, chi2 18.1/6.

The method exists, and it works on mock data
The nuclear stellar disk rotates: near side toward positive longitude, far side toward negative — a ~5 mas/yr difference against ~1 mas/yr scatter. A proper motion therefore places a star on the near or the far side, and its extinction measures the dust in front of it.

The 3D shape and orbits of the CMZ. What is constrained: the dense gas spans 1.8 > l > -1.3 deg with 70-75% at positive longitude (3D CMZ I), and every independent near/far tracer so far (H2CO absorption in III, 8/70 micron extinction in IV, star counts + X-ray echoes + proper motions combined in V, stellar proper motions in Nogueras-Lara+26) puts most clouds on the near side: 78% of the 23 sources placed by absorption. ACES maps all potentially star-forming CMZ gas

Longmore+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20340 · AG: author 6 of 98
  • ALMA Band 3 (85–102 GHz) mosaic of 45 fields over l = 359.4°–0.8°: all gas above N(H2) = 1022 cm−2; ALMA's largest contiguous map
  • Designed for ~1.5″ (0.05 pc), 0.2–3 km s−1 channels and >70 spectral features, with 7m + TP data and a matched simulation suite

ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) I: Overview. The footprint is every sightline with Herschel N(H2) ≥ 1022 cm−2 in 359.4° ≤ l ≤ 0.8°, |b| ≤ 0.25° that is kinematically tied to the ~100 pc stream. The four science objectives are: what drives mass flows as a function of scale and location, the 3D geometry, whether there are preferred locations for star formation, and whether SF theory holds in this environment. The early-science results are from other ACES papers: Nonhebel+ 2024 (M0.8−0.2 ring: r = 6.1 pc, ~21 km/s expansion, >1051 erg; a hypernova is proposed), Sofue+ 2025 (nested arms), and our MUBLO paper (Ginsburg+ 2024). The 1.5″ and 0.07 mJy/beam numbers are design targets; the delivered mosaics are ~2–3″ (Papers II–V).

The whole CMZ in 3 mm continuum at ~0.1 pc

Ginsburg+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20240 · AG: first author
  • 45 ALMA 12m mosaics, >1000 arcmin2 at ~2.5″; typical rms ~0.1 mJy/beam, much higher near Sgr A* and Sgr B2
  • Feathered with the new GBT MUSTANG-2 TENS survey (10″) and Planck to recover all angular scales
  • In-band spectral index (86.5 vs 99.5 GHz) separates dust from free-free/synchrotron only for bright, compact sources
  • Brick: a southern 3 mm excess implies substantial free-free emission missed by Herschel-based extrapolation
ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) II: 3mm continuum images. 12m-only images recover scales up to ~10–20″; the common circular beams are 2.30″ (all data), 2.80″ (86.5 GHz) and 2.14″ (99.5 GHz), and the 12m footprint is 1286 arcmin2 at low frequency. The 7m continuum is not used: MUSTANG-2 (MGPS90 plus new TENS data covering the western half) fills the short spacings better. Spectral indices toward extended emission are not trustworthy (the two estimation methods disagree, and α → −4 rings around peaks are probably a flux-recovery artifact). Comparisons with earlier ALMA data agree well, except that deeper programs with better uv coverage, or with self-calibration toward Sgr B2 and Sgr A*, do better there. In the Brick, subtracting scaled MeerKAT free-free emission brings ACES+TENS closer to the Cycle 0 + Herschel image but does not fully reconcile them.

Linear HCO+ absorption filaments pervade the CMZ

Walker+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20276 · AG: author 2 of 67
  • HNCO 4–3 and HCO+ 1–0 at 0.2 km s−1, 12m + 7m + single dish, ~2″; cubes, moment and l–v mosaics released
  • HNCO follows the dense cloud interiors; HCO+ traces more diffuse gas and is dominated by the Sgr A region
  • Many sightlines show overlapping velocity components
  • Parsec-scale linear HCO+ absorption filaments, previously reported only in the Brick, are ubiquitous; their origin is unknown
ALMA Central molecular zone Exploration Survey (ACES) III: Molecular line data reduction and HNCO and HCO+ data. This paper describes the line pipeline (calibration, continuum subtraction, feather-based array combination, and mosaicking of 45 fields with field-dependent velocity offsets). The median beam is 2.1″ and the median noise is 14 mJy/beam. ACES resolves substructure ~15× finer than Mopra and recovers ~0.7 of Mopra's integrated HNCO flux. The absorption filaments were first reported in the Brick (Bally+ 2014); ACES shows they occur throughout the CMZ, but no automated catalog exists yet.

SiO and isotopologue lines are diffuse and filamentary

Lu+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20445 · AG: author 3 of 64
  • SiO, SO, H13CO+, H13CN, HN13C and HC15N at 1.7 km s−1, ~2.6″ (0.1 pc); cubes and maps released
  • SiO, H13CO+, H13CN and HN13C are diffuse, often filamentary, and strongly inter-correlated (ρ > 0.6): possibly shock-related
  • H40α tracks the 3 mm continuum best; HNCO, HCO+ and CS correlate only weakly (ρ ≲ 0.4)
  • HC15N appears optically thin: a candidate dense-gas tracer
ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES)-IV. Data of the two intermediate-width spectral windows. The correlations are pixel-by-pixel Pearson coefficients between integrated-intensity maps and the continuum, computed with and without Sgr B2 and Sgr A. The SO–continuum correlation (0.72) is driven by Sgr B2 and drops to 0.43 without it. The weak correlation of the classic tracers is attributed to a mix of continuum not tracing dense gas (free-free, synchrotron) and lines not tracing it (optical depth, shock enhancement). The HN13C/H13CN ratio may rise toward cloud interiors, consistent with externally heated clouds; that is a possible thermometer, not yet calibrated.

CS and SO are extended; HC3N and CH3CHO compact

Hsieh+ 2026, arXiv:2603.00863 · AG: author 3 of 62
  • CS 2–1, SO, HC3N 11–10, CH3CHO and H40α at 1.5 km s−1, ~2.8″×2.1″
  • CS (likely optically thick) and SO are extended; CH3CHO is confined to the Brick, 20/50 km s−1 clouds and Sgr B
  • CH3CHO follows HC3N (ρ = 0.84–0.9), not CS
  • SO and HC3N correlate with SiO (ρ = 0.90, 0.83): likely linked to shocked gas
ALMA Central molecular zone Exploration Survey (ACES) V: CS(2-1), SO(2_3-1_2), CH3CHO(5_1,4-4_1,3), HC3N(11-10), and H40a lines data. These are the two broad windows. CS peaks near 10 K, comparable to the gas kinetic temperature, so it is probably thick across most of the CMZ; CH3CHO peaks below 1 K. The CH3CHO–HC3N correlation holds in all four zoomed regions (Brick, 20 and 50 km/s clouds, Three Little Pigs). CMZ COMs are thought to be tied to low-velocity shocks rather than to star formation, so compact CH3CHO need not mark hot cores. CS shows the known foreground absorption at −52, −28 and −3 km/s; H40α is presented but not analyzed.

The CMZ is highly filamentary at 0.1 pc resolution

Battersby+ 2026, MNRAS 550, stag1119 · AG: author 19 of 47
  • In HNCO: large filaments 10–49 pc long, and ubiquitous ~1–3 pc small ones; both ~0.1 pc wide
  • Two of the three large filaments follow the CMZ orbital streams in position–position–velocity
  • One (LF 2) resembles an extended velocity feature: a candidate inflow toward Sgr A*
  • Field alignment is mixed: one ⊥, one ∥, one mixed
ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) — VI. ALMA large program reveals a highly filamentary Central Molecular Zone. The paper analyzes three filaments of each class in detail; it is not a complete census. LF lengths are 32, 9.8 and 49 pc (projected, so lower limits); widths are 0.08–0.18 pc, except LF 3 at 0.7 pc. Line widths are ≥2× the CMZ size–linewidth expectation, so all six are probably in the CMZ. The authors suggest the large filaments are pieces of the orbital structure and could help fit 3D orbit models. The small filaments may come from turbulence and shear, as seen in simulations. The field comparison uses FIREPLACE 214 μm data, about 10× coarser than ACES.

Filament–field alignment varies across the CMZ

Paré+ 2026, ApJ 1000, 37 · AG: author 20 of 36
  • 12 large HNCO filaments (~10 pc, away from the main clouds) compared with SOFIA 214 μm and JCMT 850 μm field orientations
  • At 214 μm the relative angles are bimodal, peaking near 30° and 90°; at 850 μm they are mostly parallel
  • Strong-field MHD filaments are parallel, like the shear- or subsonic-dominated ones: tentatively, magnetically supported against collapse
  • Proposed as CMZ “ribs”; unlike disk “bones”, not mostly perpendicular
ACES: The Magnetic Field in Large Filaments in the Galactic Center. Filaments are found with the Rolling Hough Transform on ACES HNCO smoothed to the FIREPLACE beam, and alignment is measured with the AM and HRO statistics. Both field datasets (19.6″ and 12″) are coarser than the filament widths, so they may trace the surrounding field rather than the field inside the filaments; the 214/850 μm difference may reflect line-of-sight structure or dust temperature. Perpendicular filaments are read as shock or supersonic-turbulence compression, parallel ones as shear or subsonic turbulence. The MHD comparison uses only a strong-field model, so the “magnetically supported” conclusion is tentative.

235 compact high-velocity features across the CMZ

Lipman+ 2026, arXiv:2609.10472 · AG: author 13 of 48
  • 235 high-velocity compact cloud candidates in CS 2–1 with ΔV = 20–140 km s−1; spiked PV shapes are most common
  • Mean FWHM 25.8 km s−1, vs ~10 for bulk CMZ gas
  • High HNCO/SiO at the orbit edge near Sgr B2 and in dust-ridge clouds e/f: may mean slower shocks
  • A group near Sgr A* matches CND gas; some overlap the 50 km s−1 cloud: inflow candidates
ACES VIII: A Survey of Compact, High-Velocity Features Observed in CS(2-1). Of the 235 candidates, 163 were found by eye and 72 more by dendrograms on a 9× spatially downsampled cube, which makes the method incomplete below ~0.5 pc (the MUBLO is not recovered). PV shapes are 37% spike, 27% clumpy and 22% curved, plus a few velocity bridges and loops; candidate origins are cloud collisions, feedback and shear. Nine candidates within 1′ of Sgr A* follow CND rotation. Only 37 of 154 Oka+ 2022 CO HVCCs in the field overlap, mainly because ACES resolves them into smaller pieces. Two candidates coincide with earlier IMBH candidates.

Most compact 3 mm sources lie outside the densest clouds

Wallace+ 2026, ApJ 1009, 130 · AG: author 45 of 66
  • Dendrograms plus by-eye (Zooniverse) vetting: 1735 detections; 567 in a “filtered” catalog with minimal free-free/synchrotron contamination (Sgr B2 and the CND excluded)
  • Radii 0.04–0.18 pc (median 0.07 pc); sensitive to ~13.5 M☉ at 20 K

ACES. VII. Compact Continuum Source Catalog of the Central Molecular Zone. The filtered catalog requires compactness score > 0.5 and peak > 4σ, and drops known foreground sources and possible knots in extended ionized gas.
