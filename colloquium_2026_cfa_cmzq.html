Coming out of the zoom: the bar. Gas on the bar's orbits gets shocked into two dust lanes and falls toward the center -- about a solar mass per year (Sormani & Barnes 2019 measured 2.7 from CO along the lanes; Hatchfield+ 2021 argue the effective rate is lower, 0.8, because in their simulations only ~30% accretes on the first pass).

Future: gas into the CMZ

Bar-lane gas hits the CMZ at ~200 km s−1 — Sormani+ 2019  ·  ACES finds 235 such features — Lipman+ 2026  ·  simulations now match ACES’s resolution — Tress+ 2026
Lipman+ 2026 (ACES VIII), Fig. 2: 235 compact high-velocity features in ACES CS 2-1 -- 163 found by eye (magenta: confirmed by dendrograms), 72 more by dendrograms on a downsampled cube (cyan). The CMZ

$\sim10^8$ M$_\odot$ of gas in $\sim200$ pc, 10% of Galactic star formation
Image: NRAO, Ginsburg
Ginsburg+ 2018a
$>\frac{1}{3}$ of CMZ SF is in bound clusters (3-8$\times$ local)
$\sim$50% of CMZ SF occurs in the Sgr B2 cloud.

We're going to zoom in ~300× to measure star formation by counting young stars.
  • Zoom-in to the Sgr B2 cloud:
  • Most massive, actively star-forming cloud in the galaxy: 107 M⊙
  • ALMA 3mm image showing mix of dust and free-free emission
  • Where the ~50% comes from, if anyone asks:
    • Barnes+ 2017 (MNRAS, "Star formation rates and efficiencies in the Galactic Center"): CMZ total SFR = 0.09 ± 0.02 M⊙/yr averaged over IR-luminosity, YSO-counting and free-free methods; their Sgr B2 entry is 0.005 M⊙/yr from the IR, rising to 0.045 when the embedded stellar mass is taken from VLA+WMAP instead — i.e. half the CMZ total.
    • Hatchfield+ 2024 (CMZoom IV): "we find a total SFR (excluding the CND) of ~0.05 M⊙/yr, the majority of which is due to the estimate of the SFR of Sgr B2 (~0.036 M⊙/yr)" — that is ~70%, so 50% is the conservative statement.
    • Ginsburg+ 2018b ("Distributed star formation throughout the Galactic Center cloud Sgr B2") is where the 0.036 M⊙/yr itself comes from — it is the number Hatchfield adopts.
    • So the two independent totals (0.09 and 0.05) bracket the answer and both put Sgr B2 at half or more. The honest caveat is that Sgr B2's SFR depends on how you count embedded stellar mass; the IR-only route gives ~10x less.

Star formation in the CMZ does not follow the local relation
  • Nearby clouds all follow one relation, Sigma_SFR ~ Sigma_gas^2.0, with a uniform efficiency per free-fall time (eps_ff ~ 0.026)
  • Say the caveat out loud: Pokhrel+ fitted this only below 676 Msun/pc^2 (their N(H2) < 3e22 cut), and their own Fig. 3 shows eps_ff declining above ~1000. Every CMZ point here is 1–2 dex beyond the calibration — pure extrapolation, exactly as I said about Gutermuth in the 2018 paper
  • The bands are cloud-to-cloud scatter: 0.30 dex, so 2 sigma = 0.60 dex

Where each CMZ point comes from — all three limits drawn as generously as the data allow, so the gap is not overstated:

  • Sgr B2 (Ginsburg+ 2018) — the whole Fig. 17a point cloud, not one point. Fig. 17a plots Sigma_* vs Sigma_gas, so the whole panel is divided by an age to get Sigma_SFR. 556 points digitised straight out of the paper's raster. Age = 0.5 Myr is used: it matches Pokhrel's t_PS so both axes mean the same thing, and it is more conservative than the paper's own 0.74 Myr (a shorter age raises the rate). Median (log Sigma_gas, log Sigma_SFR) = (3.81, 2.08); the relation predicts 3.52, so 1.44 dex below. Age sensitivity, all still below: 1 Myr → 1.74 dex below; 0.5 Myr → 1.44; 0.1 Myr (the extreme) → 0.74 dex below, still outside 2 sigma. So the conclusion does not depend on the age. Axis calibration was checked against the figure's own Gutermuth alpha=1 model lines: at Sigma_gas = 1e4 the t = 0.74 and 0.01 Myr curves are predicted at page rows 678 and 989 and found at 677 and 993. Caveats to own: Fig. 17a assumes 95 Msun per source (21.8 Msun mean x 23% IMF sampling), the sample misses Class II sources so the stellar mass may be underestimated by up to 2x, and the right-hand column of saturated-Herschel lower limits is excluded from the cloud drawn here.
  • The Brick (Walker+ 2021), log Sigma_gas = 3.71, limit log Sigma_SFR < 1.03 — 2.28 dex below the relation, and ~1.3 dex below Sgr B2 at the same gas surface density. Cloud-scale aperture, to match how Pokhrel measure and so the two limits are comparable: >1e5 Msun within R = 2–3 pc (Sec 1); R = 2.5 pc → 19.6 pc^2 → 5.1e3 Msun/pc^2. Walker+ quote no SFR and no limit, so the ceiling is ours and deliberately generous: every Table 3 core at its 22 K mass, source 1 counted whole (64.2 Msun) not split, = 104 Msun of core gas, times a core-to-star efficiency of 1.0 — every gram already a star — / 0.5 Myr / 19.6 pc^2 → 10.6. These cores really are protostellar: 9 of 18 drive SiO outflows. Using the 19" ALMA field alone instead (7e3 Msun, 1.75 pc^2) moves it to (4.0e3, 119) — same cloud, aperture ten times smaller.
  • Cloud E/F (Barnes+ 2019 + Walker+ 2018), log Sigma_gas = 3.78, limit log Sigma_SFR < 0.29 — 3.16 dex below the relation, ~2 dex below Sgr B2. Sigma_gas from Walker+ 2018 Table 1, which tabulates the dust ridge on a consistent footing: cloud e = 11.2e4 Msun in R = 2.4 pc, cloud f = 7.3e4 Msun in R = 2.0 pc; Barnes treat them as one cloud, so 1.85e5 Msun over 30.7 pc^2 = 6.0e3 Msun/pc^2. The Immer+ 2012 column of the same table gives 2.6e3, so the horizontal position is good to a factor of ~2. The ceiling is set by what is observed: no HII regions, no protostellar source catalog, and the only star formation signature in either cloud is one H2O + Class II CH3OH maser pair in the south. A Class II methanol maser marks a high-mass protostar, so allow one plus companions, ≤ 30 Msun — generous, and bounded above by the Brick's entire counted protostellar content (104 Msun in 18 cores), since E/F shows strictly less. / 0.5 Myr / 30.7 pc^2 → 2.0.
  • The horizontal bars are the range of column density each cloud actually covers: from the CMZoom threshold N(H2) = 1e23 cm^-2 (Walker+ 2018 Sec 2.1, "designed to target all regions within the CMZ that lie above a column density threshold of ~1e23 cm^-2") = 2.2e3 Msun/pc^2, up to the densest structure in each cloud — the Brick's maser core, 72 Msun in R = 0.04 pc (Rathborne+ 2015 via Walker+ 2021) = 1.4e4 = 6.4e23 cm^-2; and Cloud E/F's peak column, 3.7e24 cm^-2 (Barnes+ 2019 Sec 3.1) = 8.3e4. The bars are drawn flat, at the cloud-averaged limit. A contour-by-contour treatment would tilt them upward to the right, since the area shrinks at higher contour — that is why Pokhrel's clouds are curves, not points. So flat is the conservative rendering at the high-column end.
  • Cross-check on which of the two is denser (Tang+ 2021, MNRAS 505, 2392, Fig. 10): their AzTEC+Herschel dust-SED fit gives one uniform N(H2) map of the whole CMZ at 7", so the Brick and Cloud E/F are measured the same way. What gas reaches Sgr A*?

Sormani & Barnes 2019; Hatchfield+ 2021; Baganoff+ 2003; Marrone+ 2007; simulations: Tress+ 2024, Feng+ 2026
About a solar mass per year comes down the bar lanes into the CMZ (Sormani & Barnes 2019: 2.7; Hatchfield+ 2021: 0.8). Sgr A* gets about a millionth of that at its Bondi radius (Baganoff+ 2003, Chandra: ~1e-6 Msun/yr of hot gas), and near the event horizon Faraday rotation limits it to 2e-9 - 2e-7 (Marrone+ 2007, with the SMA). Eight orders of magnitude. Simulations fill in the middle -- magnetic transport from the ring is at most 0.01-0.1 (Tress+ 2024, an upper limit), stellar feedback drives 5e-3 at 100 pc down to 1e-6 at 1 pc, with bursts of 1e-3 at 10 pc lasting 3-5 Myr (Feng+ 2026). So: does gas get there at all, and how? Radii for the MRI limit (~100 pc, the ring) and the horizon point (~10 R_s) are approximate placements.

The method exists, and it works on mock data
The nuclear stellar disk rotates: near side toward positive longitude, far side toward negative — a ~5 mas/yr difference against ~1 mas/yr scatter. A proper motion therefore places a star on the near or the far side, and its extinction measures the dust in front of it.
Proper motions + extinctions → a non-parametric 3D dust map, with no orbital model assumed.

The 3D shape and orbits of the CMZ. What is constrained: the dense gas spans 1.8 > l > -1.3 deg with 70-75% at positive longitude (3D CMZ I), and every independent near/far tracer so far (H2CO absorption in III, 8/70 micron extinction in IV, star counts + X-ray echoes + proper motions combined in V, stellar proper motions in Nogueras-Lara+26) puts most clouds on the near side: 78% of the 23 sources placed by absorption. What is not: the orbit. The KDL open stream scores best in both III (74% near/far accuracy, table) and IV (65% min-distance agreement), but the toy ellipse is close (55-67%), and V finds that no single x2 orbit fits; it prefers nested orbits with semi-major axes ~72-146 pc. The 20 and 50 km/s clouds are the sharpest disagreement: stellar proper motions give 43+-8 and 56+-11 pc (lower limits); 3D CMZ V gives ~31 and ~42 pc (but uses the Nogueras-Lara distances as a prior, so not independent); Mills+26 argue >10 pc, likely 15-50 pc; older models (interaction with Sgr A East and the CND) put them within ~5-20 pc; Sofue+25a puts the 20 km/s cloud on Arm III (42 pc in front) and the 50 km/s cloud either near Sgr A or on Arm V (~8 pc). Proper motions are the direct test: Henshaw+16 proposed maser proper motions; Nogueras-Lara+26 is the first stellar proper-motion placement and calls itself a pilot for the whole CMZ, which is where Roman comes in. Properties of H2O Masers and Their Associated Sources in Sagittarius B2. Velocities span -39 to 172 km/s. The remaining 6% of masers are unclassified but likely protostellar. ACES maps all potentially star-forming CMZ gas

Longmore+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20340 · AG: author 6 of 98
  • ALMA Band 3 (85–102 GHz) mosaic of 45 fields over l = 359.4°–0.8°: all gas above N(H2) = 1022 cm−2; ALMA's largest contiguous map
  • Designed for ~1.5″ (0.05 pc), 0.2–3 km s−1 channels and >70 spectral features, with 7m + TP data and a matched simulation suite
  • Early results: a ~106 M☉ expanding shocked ring (M0.8−0.2), nested gas “arms”, and the MUBLO

ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) I: Overview. The footprint is every sightline with Herschel N(H2) ≥ 1022 cm−2 in 359.4° ≤ l ≤ 0.8°, |b| ≤ 0.25° that is kinematically tied to the ~100 pc stream. The four science objectives are: what drives mass flows as a function of scale and location, the 3D geometry, whether there are preferred locations for star formation, and whether SF theory holds in this environment. The early-science results are from other ACES papers: Nonhebel+ 2024 (M0.8−0.2 ring: r = 6.1 pc, ~21 km/s expansion, >1051 erg; a hypernova is proposed), Sofue+ 2025 (nested arms), and our MUBLO paper (Ginsburg+ 2024). The 1.5″ and 0.07 mJy/beam numbers are design targets; the delivered mosaics are ~2–3″ (Papers II–V).

The whole CMZ in 3 mm continuum at ~0.1 pc

Ginsburg+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20240 · AG: first author
  • 45 ALMA 12m mosaics, >1000 arcmin2 at ~2.5″; typical rms ~0.1 mJy/beam, much higher near Sgr A* and Sgr B2
  • Feathered with the new GBT MUSTANG-2 TENS survey (10″) and Planck to recover all angular scales
  • In-band spectral index (86.5 vs 99.5 GHz) separates dust from free-free/synchrotron only for bright, compact sources
  • Brick: a southern 3 mm excess implies substantial free-free emission missed by Herschel-based extrapolation
ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) II: 3mm continuum images. 12m-only images recover scales up to ~10–20″; the common circular beams are 2.30″ (all data), 2.80″ (86.5 GHz) and 2.14″ (99.5 GHz), and the 12m footprint is 1286 arcmin2 at low frequency. The 7m continuum is not used: MUSTANG-2 (MGPS90 plus new TENS data covering the western half) fills the short spacings better. Spectral indices toward extended emission are not trustworthy (the two estimation methods disagree, and α → −4 rings around peaks are probably a flux-recovery artifact). Comparisons with earlier ALMA data agree well, except that deeper programs with better uv coverage, or with self-calibration toward Sgr B2 and Sgr A*, do better there. In the Brick, subtracting scaled MeerKAT free-free emission brings ACES+TENS closer to the Cycle 0 + Herschel image but does not fully reconcile them.

Linear HCO+ absorption filaments pervade the CMZ

Walker+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20276 · AG: author 2 of 67
  • HNCO 4–3 and HCO+ 1–0 at 0.2 km s−1, 12m + 7m + single dish, ~2″; cubes, moment and l–v mosaics released
  • HNCO follows the dense cloud interiors; HCO+ traces more diffuse gas and is dominated by the Sgr A region
  • Many sightlines show overlapping velocity components
  • Parsec-scale linear HCO+ absorption filaments, previously reported only in the Brick, are ubiquitous; their origin is unknown
ALMA Central molecular zone Exploration Survey (ACES) III: Molecular line data reduction and HNCO and HCO+ data. This paper describes the line pipeline (calibration, continuum subtraction, feather-based array combination, and mosaicking of 45 fields with field-dependent velocity offsets). The median beam is 2.1″ and the median noise is 14 mJy/beam. ACES resolves substructure ~15× finer than Mopra and recovers ~0.7 of Mopra's integrated HNCO flux. The absorption filaments were first reported in the Brick (Bally+ 2014); ACES shows they occur throughout the CMZ, but no automated catalog exists yet.

SiO and isotopologue lines are diffuse and filamentary

Lu+ 2026, arXiv:2602.20445 · AG: author 3 of 64
  • SiO, SO, H13CO+, H13CN, HN13C and HC15N at 1.7 km s−1, ~2.6″ (0.1 pc); cubes and maps released
  • SiO, H13CO+, H13CN and HN13C are diffuse, often filamentary, and strongly inter-correlated (ρ > 0.6): possibly shock-related
  • H40α tracks the 3 mm continuum best; HNCO, HCO+ and CS correlate only weakly (ρ ≲ 0.4)
  • HC15N appears optically thin: a candidate dense-gas tracer
ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES)-IV. Data of the two intermediate-width spectral windows. The correlations are pixel-by-pixel Pearson coefficients between integrated-intensity maps and the continuum, computed with and without Sgr B2 and Sgr A. The SO–continuum correlation (0.72) is driven by Sgr B2 and drops to 0.43 without it. The weak correlation of the classic tracers is attributed to a mix of continuum not tracing dense gas (free-free, synchrotron) and lines not tracing it (optical depth, shock enhancement). The HN13C/H13CN ratio may rise toward cloud interiors, consistent with externally heated clouds; that is a possible thermometer, not yet calibrated.

CS and SO are extended; HC3N and CH3CHO compact

Hsieh+ 2026, arXiv:2603.00863 · AG: author 3 of 62

ALMA Central molecular zone Exploration Survey (ACES) V: CS(2-1), SO(2_3-1_2), CH3CHO(5_1,4-4_1,3), HC3N(11-10), and H40a lines data. These are the two broad windows. CS peaks near 10 K, comparable to the gas kinetic temperature, so it is probably thick across most of the CMZ; CH3CHO peaks below 1 K. The CH3CHO–HC3N correlation holds in all four zoomed regions (Brick, 20 and 50 km/s clouds, Three Little Pigs). CMZ COMs are thought to be tied to low-velocity shocks rather than to star formation, so compact CH3CHO need not mark hot cores. CS shows the known foreground absorption at −52, −28 and −3 km/s; H40α is presented but not analyzed.

The CMZ is highly filamentary at 0.1 pc resolution

Battersby+ 2026, MNRAS 550, stag1119 · AG: author 19 of 47
  • In HNCO: large filaments 10–49 pc long, and ubiquitous ~1–3 pc small ones; both ~0.1 pc wide
  • Two of the three large filaments follow the CMZ orbital streams in position–position–velocity
  • One (LF 2) resembles an extended velocity feature: a candidate inflow toward Sgr A*
  • Field alignment is mixed: one ⊥, one ∥, one mixed
ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) — VI. ALMA large program reveals a highly filamentary Central Molecular Zone. The paper analyzes three filaments of each class in detail; it is not a complete census. LF lengths are 32, 9.8 and 49 pc (projected, so lower limits); widths are 0.08–0.18 pc, except LF 3 at 0.7 pc. Line widths are ≥2× the CMZ size–linewidth expectation, so all six are probably in the CMZ. The authors suggest the large filaments are pieces of the orbital structure and could help fit 3D orbit models. The small filaments may come from turbulence and shear, as seen in simulations. The field comparison uses FIREPLACE 214 μm data, about 10× coarser than ACES.

Filament–field alignment varies across the CMZ

Paré+ 2026, ApJ 1000, 37 · AG: author 20 of 36
  • 12 large HNCO filaments (~10 pc, away from the main clouds) compared with SOFIA 214 μm and JCMT 850 μm field orientations
  • At 214 μm the relative angles are bimodal, peaking near 30° and 90°; at 850 μm they are mostly parallel
  • Strong-field MHD filaments are parallel, like the shear- or subsonic-dominated ones: tentatively, magnetically supported against collapse
  • Proposed as CMZ “ribs”; unlike disk “bones”, not mostly perpendicular
ACES: The Magnetic Field in Large Filaments in the Galactic Center. Filaments are found with the Rolling Hough Transform on ACES HNCO smoothed to the FIREPLACE beam, and alignment is measured with the AM and HRO statistics. Both field datasets (19.6″ and 12″) are coarser than the filament widths, so they may trace the surrounding field rather than the field inside the filaments; the 214/850 μm difference may reflect line-of-sight structure or dust temperature. Perpendicular filaments are read as shock or supersonic-turbulence compression, parallel ones as shear or subsonic turbulence. The MHD comparison uses only a strong-field model, so the “magnetically supported” conclusion is tentative.

235 compact high-velocity features across the CMZ

Lipman+ 2026, arXiv:2609.10472 · AG: author 13 of 48

  • Mean FWHM 25.8 km s−1, vs ~10 for bulk CMZ gas
  • High HNCO/SiO at the orbit edge near Sgr B2 and in dust-ridge clouds e/f: may mean slower shocks

ACES VIII: A Survey of Compact, High-Velocity Features Observed in CS(2-1). Of the 235 candidates, 163 were found by eye and 72 more by dendrograms on a 9× spatially downsampled cube, which makes the method incomplete below ~0.5 pc (the MUBLO is not recovered). Wallace+ 2026, ApJ 1009, 130 · AG: author 45 of 66

ACES. VII. Compact Continuum Source Catalog of the Central Molecular Zone.
