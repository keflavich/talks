What do we want from a star formation theory?

As observers, we want it to
  • Predict the appearance of young stellar objects (YSOs)
    • SED & morphological models
  • Predict how common each type (or phase) of YSO is
    • Accretion history, lifetime
  • ... and thereby let us infer the mass distribution and history of star formation from an observed sample
    • IMF, SFH
It's not there yet.

What do we want from a star formation theory?

More broadly, we want it to predict
  • $L_*(t, \vec{x})$
  • $M_*(t, \vec{x})$
  • $\dot{M}_*(t, \vec{x})$

Some closing thoughts on what we've learned of star formation:
  • steady-state models have been favored because they're easier
  • turbulence models (steady state) are appealingly simple and seem to match extragalactic data, but they are wrong in every detail
  • quantitative studies with ALMA and our first glimpses with JWST, compounding years of data and models, point toward the importance of short-timescale, accelerating or stochastic processes in determining where and how much feedback gets deposited.
Other future directions: dynamics = proper motions within the dense gas

The characteristic fragmentation scale


The Jeans Mass MJ is the mass where gravity and thermal pressure are balanced.

MJ ∝ T3/2 ρ−1/2
  • Higher density means greater gravity, leading to collapse and fragmentation
  • Higher temperature means greater pressure, providing support against gravitational collapse and suppressing it.

The characteristic fragmentation scale is larger
